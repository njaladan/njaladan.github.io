---
title:  "Artemis"
---

*Artemis* is a fine science fiction flick from Andy Weir, who also wrote *The Martian*. This book was good! In most cases, it’s standard; I didn’t feel too much powerful character development. It wasn’t much of a priority to the author. However, *Artemis* shines when it comes to the classic hard science fiction portrayals of lunar living. The descriptions and thought placed into constructing the alternative world are frankly awesome, especially because the stories that he writes are often set in the not-too-distant future, which makes them quite relatable.

There’s a deliberate shift in tone compared to *The Martian*, however. While the latter was a hard science fiction story set with the primary intention of giving breathtaking descriptions of Mars and potentially living on the planet with constrained resources, *Artemis* takes a more free-flowing approach, dabbling in adventurous buccaneering, character development, and relationships between characters. There’s a lot going on in terms of character relationships and motivations. While the characters themselves don’t change much, I like the strong personalities that are established. It sucks that the primary conflict wasn’t a result of conflicting characters with different motivations (an evil mafia is probably the farthest you can get from that), but having a villain be someone like the mayor woman or the dad would be interesting, since those were the people that had developed character intentions that the protagonist conflicted with.

On another note, it was weird how sexual the book was, and how little it added to the plot. Part of it was to demonstrate Jazz’s relationship with her conservative religious dad and to show how they contrasted as people, but other than that it felt weird. The suggested audience for *Artemis* is older than that of the author’s prior book. Also, while I loved the scientific descriptions of objects and situations in the book, the logical structure could have used some work. The main character leaks chloroform into the living space, almost killing everyone—but then *literally everyone in the town passes it off as a joke*. This was disturbing. I know she saved them from a potential soft mafia takeover, but treating her like a hero after that is insane. There are easier ways to establish the heroic character than that. Perhaps everyone’s “death” and subsequent “rebirth” could have been symbolic, but it wasn’t like that in this case.

Overall, a solid thriller sci-fi that’s great for a light read. I wouldn’t read this again, but thinking back the characters were endearing. I’ll look forward to the inevitable movie adaptation...

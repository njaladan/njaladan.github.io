# Travel

Static map at `/travel/`. Serve with `python -m http.server 8765 --directory travel`.

One persistent Leaflet map supports scroll zoom, dragging, touch gestures, and animated navigation. Only the confirmed visited countries and US home are selectable. China is scoped to Hong Kong. Markers make Hong Kong and Vatican City accessible on the world map. The World map button zooms back out.

Routes, city markers, and future photo pins belong on the map. There are no splash sections, itineraries, country lists, side panels, or example activity records. All visits to a country appear together. Activity data is in `data/activities.json`; city locations are in `data.js`. France, Italy, and Switzerland open their own maps. TMB gets a shared route view only when actual exports are present. No Strava links are included.

Country and state boundaries, visited places, recorded activity tracks, and transport paths are packaged locally. The neutral basemap uses white water, almost-white land, gray outlines, and high-level labels. Visited countries have a terracotta fill, with sage green for the US home; the fills fade between zoom levels 3.5 and 5 so close views remain neutral; it has no terrain or elevation layer. At regional/city zoom, a grayscale Esri Light Gray street basemap fades in to show roads, rivers, parks, nearby places, and landmarks. Detail tiles require network access; the packaged overview and travel overlays work independently of the provider. Background labels yield to visited-place labels. Country selection uses local geometry and an animated camera. Country clicks focus on recorded visits: nearby places are weighted by activity count, with each activity contributing once rather than by GPS point count. A neighborhood containing at least 60% of the visit/activity weight gets the initial focus; otherwise all recorded areas are framed. UK opens around London. Flights and cross-country transport endpoints do not affect focus. Pan and zoom remain free, without a whole-country button; the US initially opens at the national level. France uses metropolitan geometry for selection. Hong Kong SAR uses its own geometry. Leaflet and TopoJSON retain their upstream license headers in `vendor/`.

## Imported activity data

The Netherlands ride is from the user-provided `11393102827.gpx`: May 12, 2024, 11,214 GPS points, Amsterdam to Utrecht. The five named places are user-confirmed visits, with place-center markers; they are not linked by an invented travel route.

The reviewed local Strava export adds 59 international activities, for 60 recorded activities across 13 countries/regions. Original titles, local dates, activity types, distance, elapsed time, and ascent are retained. The seven TMB recordings include both parts of day six; two additional Bourg-Saint-Maurice hikes stay separate. Records without GPS are excluded. Existing Amsterdam data is preserved without duplication.

Import a reviewed JSON manifest with `python travel/scripts/import_strava.py EXPORT_DIRECTORY REVIEW.json` (FIT input requires `fitdecode`). Manifest entries select activities by `Activity ID` and retain the reviewed `Filename`, `date`, and `countries`. Strava CSV IDs are authoritative even when original FIT filenames have different IDs. FIT tracks are cropped to the CSV activity start and elapsed interval, since original files can include driving removed from the published hike. Track segments retain gaps; display geometry is simplified within 3 meters with segment endpoints preserved. Export statistics are not recalculated from the simplified geometry. Reimports skip existing activity IDs by default; `--replace` refreshes newly reviewed tracks while preserving activities marked `already_imported`.

For the original GPX importer, distance is calculated from consecutive GPS points within each recorded segment, elapsed time comes from track timestamps, and ascent is an estimate from positive altitude changes, shown with an approximate sign. New bulk imports use the statistics from the Strava CSV instead.

Import another GPX with `python travel/scripts/import_gpx.py TRACK.gpx --journey netherlands --name "Amsterdam ride" --countries 528 --timezone Europe/Amsterdam`. Existing activity IDs are replaced on reimport. GPX track gaps remain separate segments.

TMB exports can use `--journey tmb --name "Tour du Mont Blanc" --countries 250,380,756 --timezone Europe/Paris`. TMB imports are checked against the Mont Blanc area. Routes show together by default and can be selected directly on the map.

When changing the app or data, advance the asset version in `index.html` so GitHub Pages caches cannot mix old code with new markup or activity data.

`data/context.json` contains 185 high-level Natural Earth city labels (about 10 KB). It contains background locations, not travel records. Unused road, river, and lake geometry and lower-priority cities have been removed. Rebuild it with `python travel/scripts/build_context.py SOURCE_DIRECTORY` from `ne_50m_populated_places.geojson`. Country and state label locations come from packaged outlines. Street-level context comes from the separate grayscale detail tiles rather than this dataset.

Transport connections in `data/transport.json` retain ticket-backed dates/operators and user-confirmed stops. Train lines follow BRouter’s OpenStreetMap rail network; bus and driving lines follow OSRM’s OpenStreetMap roads. Their colors distinguish train, bus, drive, hike, walk, bike, and ferry. All transport paths are bundled, so viewing them never calls a routing service. Recorded GPS tracks are stored separately in `data/activities.json`. Public data excludes personal addresses, email identifiers, booking references, payment details, and airport-only layovers. The California-to-Seattle road trip route follows the user-provided itinerary. Rebuild transport paths with `python travel/scripts/build_transport_routes.py CACHE_DIRECTORY`; the cache avoids repeating successful routing requests. Endpoint platform pins and selected rail waypoints prevent snapping to disconnected tram tracks.

Iceland has eleven driving legs connecting Reykjavík, all ten recorded mainland hike stops in recording order, Hvolsvöllur, and Landeyjahöfn. Each leg meets the corrected GPS start/end points with short parking/trailhead access connectors, while its main path follows roads. The Heimaey hike stays on the island and is reached by the separate Herjólfur ferry, with ticket dates October 4–5, 2025. Rebuild only these roads with `python travel/scripts/build_iceland_routes.py CACHE_DIRECTORY`; coordinate fingerprints prevent an edited hike endpoint from reusing a stale cached route.

Confirmed ferry definitions live in the readable `data/ferries.json` and are merged into transport with `python travel/scripts/build_ferry_routes.py`. They cover Landeyjahöfn ↔ Heimaey, Athens/Piraeus → Syros → Naxos → Athens/Piraeus, Minori → Salerno, Positano → Salerno, and Paine Grande → Pudeto. Dates/operators are from confirmations; the three Greek crossings are also explicitly user-confirmed. Iceland uses OpenStreetMap's mapped Herjólfur sea geometry. Other paths are inferred port-to-port sea/lake crossings, shaped around coastlines; they are not recorded vessel GPS. The Greek paths have been checked against Natural Earth coastlines to avoid crossing islands or Attica. Ferry paths appear in blue with dashes and their own legend entry. Rebuilding transport preserves ferry definitions and the hike-based Iceland road legs.

Five driving paths reconstruct the August 2026 Jordan and Tunisia trips at the user's request. They use dashed Drive lines, with estimate/source notes retained in the data rather than the popups. They use road geometry rather than GPS recordings. Jordan's road order is Aqaba → Wadi Rum → Wadi Musa (Petra) → Wadi Mujib Adventure Center → Dead Sea → Amman, based on arrival, tour, hotel, and user-confirmed stop details for August 25–28. The routes follow OpenStreetMap roads and stop in Wadi Musa rather than tracing roads through the archaeological site. No Jerash or desert jeep excursions are added.

Tunisia's August 30–31 tour uses the booked Tunis-airport pickup/return and a [published itinerary matching the booked tour title](https://www.kkday.com/en/product/806296). Day 1 runs through Kairouan, Matmata, and Tamezret to Ksar Ghilane; Day 2 returns through Tataouine, Chenini, Ksar Haddada, and El Jem. These intermediate stops and their order are itinerary-based estimates, not independently confirmed visits. Existing city markers are preserved. Rebuild just these routes with `python travel/scripts/import_estimated_drives.py CACHE_DIRECTORY`; rerunning replaces the same route IDs without duplicating them.

Background outlines remain neutral at every zoom. Visited-place labels and routes become visible as the camera zooms in. Wheel, trackpad, and zoom buttons interpolate a fractional camera; touch pinch does not snap to fixed levels. Reduced-motion navigation fits the target immediately.

## Flights

The user-provided flights are shown as approximate airport connections, with flight details available in route popups. Airport arrivals/departures do not add city, state, or country visit claims.

Faint dashed blue lines are approximate great-circle connections, not recorded flight paths. Lines brighten on hover, keyboard focus, or popup selection, while airports stay subdued. Explanatory source notes are kept in data/documentation rather than repeated in popups. Dateline crossings are split to avoid drawing lines across the map. Airport coordinates come from the public-domain OurAirports dataset, with the required entries saved in `data/airports.json`. No airport lookup or routing service is called by the browser. Reimport with `python travel/scripts/import_flights.py PATH_TO_FLIGHTS.csv`; add coordinates to `data/airports.json` first when an export introduces a new airport. The uploaded CSV stays outside the public repository; seat numbers, registrations, notes, and internal export IDs are not published.

US boundaries are packaged from us-atlas (US Census geometry). All 50 states can be explored, with 22 user-confirmed visited states, their cities/areas, and 12 national parks in `US_VISITS`. These are independent place markers; the California-to-Seattle drive is drawn from the user-provided itinerary. State outlines appear as the camera zooms in. Visited-city labels use screen-space collision detection and alternative placements, and clicking a city does not open a duplicate name popup. Giza is omitted at the user’s request.
